# 4. Diagnostic EP Maneuvers

## 4.1 Core Principles
- Explain the purpose of diagnostic electrophysiology (EP) maneuvers.
- Differentiate diagnostic pacing from therapeutic pacing.
- Correlate intracardiac electrograms with the surface ECG.
- Distinguish antegrade from retrograde conduction.
- Explain how pacing site, cycle length, and stimulus timing affect results.
- Recognize the effects of medications, autonomic tone, and sedation.
- Interpret findings using multiple complementary maneuvers.
- Document measurements, observations, and diagnostic uncertainty.
- Follow procedural safety protocols under appropriate supervision.

## 4.2 Incremental Atrial Pacing
- Define incremental atrial pacing and its diagnostic purpose.
- Describe pacing at progressively shorter cycle lengths.
- Confirm atrial capture and assess subsequent His and ventricular activation.
- Measure AH interval changes as pacing rates increase.
- Recognize decremental AV nodal conduction and progressive AH prolongation.
- Identify AV nodal block and differentiate it from failure of atrial capture.
- Explain how atrial pacing can reveal dual AV nodal physiology.
- Assess accessory-pathway conduction when indicated.
- Recognize rate-dependent aberrancy and conduction abnormalities.
- Interpret pacing responses alongside baseline conduction measurements.

## 4.3 Incremental Ventricular Pacing
- Define incremental ventricular pacing and its clinical applications.
- Assess retrograde conduction from the ventricles to the atria.
- Identify ventricular, His-bundle, and atrial activation sequences.
- Differentiate concentric from eccentric retrograde atrial activation.
- Recognize decremental conduction and possible accessory-pathway participation.
- Identify ventriculoatrial block and rate-dependent conduction changes.
- Explain how ventricular pacing assists in evaluating AVRT and other tachycardias.
- Recognize that activation sequence alone does not definitively establish a mechanism.
- Account for pacing location, catheter position, and recording quality.

## 4.4 AV Nodal Conduction
- Explain the role of the AV node in regulating atrial impulses reaching the ventricles.
- Measure AH intervals during sinus rhythm and atrial pacing.
- Recognize decremental conduction and progressive AH prolongation.
- Differentiate AV nodal from infranodal conduction block using intracardiac recordings.
- Explain dual AV nodal physiology and its relationship to AVNRT.
- Describe the effects of autonomic tone and medications on AV nodal conduction.
- Recognize the limitations of individual conduction measurements.
- Integrate findings with baseline ECG and intracardiac recordings.

## 4.5 AV Nodal Wenckebach Cycle Length
- Define the AV nodal Wenckebach cycle length.
- Explain how incremental atrial pacing identifies progressive AV nodal delay.
- Identify the pacing cycle length at which consistent AV nodal conduction fails.
- Recognize progressive AH prolongation before a nonconducted atrial impulse.
- Differentiate AV nodal block from infranodal block and failed capture.
- Explain how medications and autonomic conditions affect Wenckebach behavior.
- Document pacing conditions and interpret results in clinical context.

## 4.6 Atrial Effective Refractory Period
- Define the atrial effective refractory period (AERP).
- Explain the drive train and premature atrial extrastimulus protocol.
- Describe the S1-S2 coupling interval and progressive shortening.
- Identify the shortest coupling interval producing atrial capture under the defined protocol.
- Differentiate failure of atrial capture from failure of AV conduction.
- Explain how pacing site, cycle length, medications, and autonomic tone affect AERP.
- Relate atrial refractoriness to arrhythmia initiation without treating it as a standalone predictor.
- Document stimulation site, protocol, and capture criteria.

## 4.7 Ventricular Effective Refractory Period
- Define the ventricular effective refractory period (VERP).
- Explain the programmed ventricular stimulation protocol.
- Identify the shortest coupling interval producing ventricular capture under the defined protocol.
- Differentiate failure of capture from failure of subsequent conduction.
- Explain how pacing site, drive cycle length, medications, and electrolytes affect VERP.
- Relate ventricular refractoriness to reentrant arrhythmia mechanisms.
- Recognize that VERP alone cannot reliably predict spontaneous ventricular arrhythmia risk.
- Document the stimulation protocol and relevant findings.

## 4.8 Premature Stimulation
- Explain how premature atrial and ventricular stimuli assess conduction and refractoriness.
- Describe the use of extrastimuli to initiate or reset tachycardias.
- Explain how premature atrial stimuli may initiate AVNRT.
- Describe how premature ventricular stimuli may reveal accessory-pathway participation.
- Explain the diagnostic use of His-refractory ventricular premature beats.
- Recognize changes in activation sequence and conduction block after extrastimuli.
- Differentiate arrhythmia initiation from evidence identifying its sustaining mechanism.
- Interpret results alongside other pacing maneuvers.

## 4.9 Ventricular Overdrive Pacing
- Define ventricular overdrive pacing during tachycardia.
- Explain pacing faster than the tachycardia cycle length.
- Confirm ventricular capture and assess entrainment.
- Measure the post-pacing interval when reliable signals are available.
- Compare the post-pacing interval with the tachycardia cycle length.
- Assess the sequence of ventricular and atrial activation after pacing stops.
- Explain the significance and limitations of V-A-V responses.
- Use findings to help differentiate AVNRT, AVRT, and atrial tachycardia.
- Recognize the effects of fusion, conduction delay, and pacing-site distance.

## 4.10 Atrial Overdrive Pacing
- Define atrial overdrive pacing during tachycardia.
- Explain pacing faster than the tachycardia cycle length.
- Assess atrial capture, resetting, entrainment, acceleration, and termination.
- Examine atrial, His-bundle, and ventricular activation sequences.
- Explain the significance and limitations of A-A-V responses.
- Assess whether atrial activity drives ventricular activation.
- Recognize how AV block during pacing may provide diagnostic information.
- Integrate responses with other EP findings rather than relying on one feature.

## 4.11 Differential Pacing Maneuvers
- Explain the purpose of differential atrial and ventricular pacing.
- Compare activation sequences from different pacing sites.
- Describe para-Hisian pacing for selected assessments of retrograde conduction.
- Differentiate AV nodal conduction from accessory-pathway conduction when supported by pacing responses.
- Explain the role of His-refractory ventricular premature beats.
- Assess accessory-pathway participation in suspected AVRT.
- Recognize the effects of pacing output, capture, catheter position, and concealed conduction.
- Document the maneuver and avoid conclusions unsupported by the findings.

## 4.12 Distinguishing AVNRT, AVRT, and Atrial Tachycardia
- Explain the mechanisms underlying AVNRT, AVRT, and atrial tachycardia.
- Compare atrial, His-bundle, and ventricular activation sequences.
- Evaluate tachycardia cycle length and PR/RP or VA relationships.
- Identify evidence supporting dual AV nodal physiology and AVNRT.
- Assess accessory-pathway participation using appropriate pacing maneuvers.
- Determine whether atrial activation drives the tachycardia or forms part of a reentrant circuit.
- Explain how AV block during tachycardia may support an atrial tachycardia mechanism.
- Interpret entrainment, post-pacing intervals, resetting, and termination responses.
- Recognize that atypical mechanisms may produce overlapping findings.
- Integrate multiple maneuvers before assigning a definitive diagnosis.

## 4.13 Mechanism-Specific Findings
- **AVNRT:** Assess dual AV nodal physiology, AH jump, initiation patterns, and atrial activation timing.
- **AVRT:** Evaluate accessory-pathway conduction, ventricular pacing responses, and premature ventricular stimulation.
- **Atrial tachycardia:** Assess atrial activation sequence, atrial driving activity, and responses to atrial pacing.
- Recognize that individual findings may be suggestive rather than definitive.
- Differentiate tachycardia initiation from evidence identifying the sustaining circuit.
- Consider alternative explanations when EP findings conflict.

## 4.14 Documentation, Safety, and Competency
- Verify electrogram timing, pacing capture, and recording quality.
- Document pacing sites, cycle lengths, coupling intervals, and relevant measurements.
- Distinguish direct observations from mechanistic interpretations.
- Recognize how medication, sedation, and autonomic conditions influence results.
- Identify limitations and report uncertainty clearly.
- Demonstrate understanding of incremental pacing, Wenckebach cycle length, and refractory-period measurement.
- Interpret atrial and ventricular overdrive pacing responses.
- Use differential pacing to assess suspected arrhythmia mechanisms.
- Follow institutional protocols and appropriate procedural supervision.

## Expected Learning Outcome
Demonstrate foundational competence in diagnostic EP pacing maneuvers, conduction assessment, refractory-period measurement, and interpretation of responses used to differentiate AVNRT, AVRT, and atrial tachycardia.
