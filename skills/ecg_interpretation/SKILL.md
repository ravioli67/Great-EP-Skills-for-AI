# 2. ECG Interpretation and Arrhythmia Diagnosis

## 2.1 Systematic 12-Lead ECG Interpretation
- Verify ECG calibration, paper speed, lead placement, and recording quality.
- Assess heart rate, rhythm regularity, and P-wave-to-QRS relationships.
- Measure PR interval, QRS duration, and QT/QTc.
- Evaluate cardiac axis, waveform morphology, and ST-T abnormalities.
- Identify conduction blocks, ectopic beats, and pre-excitation.
- Correlate ECG findings with clinical history and symptoms.
- Recognize artifact and limitations of automated interpretation.

## 2.2 Atrial Fibrillation
- Recognize absent consistent sinus P waves and an irregularly irregular ventricular rhythm.
- Explain disorganized atrial activation and variable AV conduction.
- Differentiate atrial fibrillation from flutter and other irregular rhythms.
- Identify slow and rapid ventricular responses.
- Recognize pre-excited atrial fibrillation as a potentially dangerous rhythm.
- Assess clinical stability and the potential risk of thromboembolic complications.

## 2.3 Atrial Flutter
- Recognize flutter waves and typical sawtooth morphology when present.
- Identify fixed and variable AV conduction ratios.
- Differentiate atrial flutter from atrial fibrillation and focal atrial tachycardia.
- Explain the macroreentrant mechanism of typical atrial flutter.
- Recognize flutter waves concealed within QRS complexes or T waves.
- Describe the role of electrophysiology studies and catheter ablation.

## 2.4 Atrioventricular Nodal Reentrant Tachycardia (AVNRT)
- Explain reentry involving functionally distinct pathways in or near the AV node.
- Recognize regular narrow-complex tachycardia with potentially hidden retrograde P waves.
- Identify typical short-RP tachycardia patterns.
- Explain dual AV nodal physiology and initiation by premature atrial beats.
- Differentiate AVNRT from AVRT and focal atrial tachycardia.
- Describe the diagnostic roles of pacing maneuvers and intracardiac recordings.
- Explain the principles of slow-pathway catheter ablation.

## 2.5 Atrioventricular Reentrant Tachycardia (AVRT)
- Explain how an accessory pathway participates in a reentrant circuit.
- Differentiate orthodromic AVRT from antidromic AVRT.
- Recognize typical narrow-complex and wide-complex presentations.
- Identify ECG features of manifest pre-excitation, including a short PR interval and delta wave.
- Understand concealed accessory pathways.
- Recognize the danger of pre-excited atrial fibrillation.
- Explain the role of electrophysiologic testing and catheter ablation.


# 2.6 Focal Atrial Tachycardia
- Explain focal atrial tachycardia mechanisms, including abnormal automaticity, triggered activity, and localized reentry.
- Recognize regular atrial activity with P waves differing from sinus morphology.
- Assess atrial rate, ventricular rate, and AV conduction.
- Differentiate focal atrial tachycardia from sinus tachycardia, atrial flutter, AVNRT, and AVRT.
- Recognize warm-up and cool-down patterns while understanding their diagnostic limitations.
- Explain how AV block may help identify an atrial tachycardia mechanism.
- Describe the roles of intracardiac mapping and catheter ablation.

# 2.7 Supraventricular Tachycardia Versus Ventricular Tachycardia
- Differentiate supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) from ventricular tachycardia (VT).
- Assess QRS width, rhythm regularity, P waves, and atrioventricular relationships.
- Recognize AV dissociation, capture beats, and fusion beats as findings supporting VT.
- Evaluate QRS morphology, axis, and precordial concordance.
- Consider SVT with aberrancy, pre-excitation, and ventricular pacing in wide-complex differential diagnosis.
- Recognize that undifferentiated wide-complex tachycardia must be treated as potentially VT until adequately evaluated.
- Apply validated ECG algorithms and integrate the patient's clinical history.
- Prioritize hemodynamic stability and urgent assessment when indicated.

# 2.8 Premature Atrial Complexes
- Define premature atrial complexes (PACs).
- Identify early P waves with morphology different from the underlying sinus P waves.
- Recognize conducted, aberrantly conducted, and nonconducted PACs.
- Differentiate PACs from premature junctional complexes and PVCs.
- Identify PACs concealed within T waves.
- Explain how PACs may reset the sinus node and alter subsequent RR intervals.
- Describe the potential role of PACs in initiating atrial fibrillation and other atrial tachyarrhythmias.
- Assess ectopic frequency, symptoms, and relevant clinical context.

# 2.9 Premature Ventricular Complexes
- Define premature ventricular complexes (PVCs).
- Recognize premature, usually wide QRS complexes without a preceding conducted P wave.
- Assess PVC morphology, coupling interval, and compensatory pauses.
- Differentiate unifocal from multifocal PVCs.
- Identify ventricular bigeminy, trigeminy, couplets, and interpolated PVCs.
- Recognize fusion complexes and differentiate PVCs from aberrantly conducted PACs.
- Explain how the site of ventricular origin influences QRS morphology.
- Evaluate frequent or complex ventricular ectopy in the context of symptoms and structural heart disease.
- Explain when ambulatory monitoring and cardiac imaging may be indicated.

# 2.10 Ventricular Tachycardia
- Define ventricular tachycardia (VT) and explain its ventricular origin.
- Differentiate monomorphic VT from polymorphic VT.
- Distinguish sustained from nonsustained VT using episode duration.
- Recognize regular wide-complex tachycardia and assess supporting ECG features.
- Identify AV dissociation, capture beats, and fusion beats.
- Explain scar-related reentry and other mechanisms of ventricular arrhythmias.
- Recognize torsades de pointes and its association with prolonged QT.
- Identify potential contributors, including ischemia, cardiomyopathy, electrolyte disturbances, and medications.
- Assess symptoms and signs of hemodynamic instability.
- Describe the roles of ECG monitoring, cardiac imaging, and electrophysiology studies.
- Explain the clinical roles of antiarrhythmic therapy, catheter ablation, and implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs).
- Recognize sustained VT as potentially life-threatening and requiring urgent clinical assessment.

# 2.11 Ventricular Fibrillation
- Define ventricular fibrillation (VF) and explain its disorganized ventricular electrical activity.
- Recognize the absence of organized, consistent QRS complexes.
- Differentiate VF from polymorphic VT, asystole, and ECG artifact.
- Explain why VF causes loss of effective cardiac output.
- Recognize VF as a cardiac arrest rhythm requiring immediate emergency response.
- Describe the importance of prompt CPR and defibrillation according to resuscitation protocols.
- Identify potential reversible contributors, including acute ischemia, electrolyte abnormalities, and drug toxicity.
- Explain the role of post-arrest evaluation in identifying causes and preventing recurrence.
- Distinguish VF from pulseless electrical activity, which has electrical activity without a palpable pulse.
- Recognize that VF requires emergency resuscitation rather than routine ECG assessment.

# 2.12 Bradyarrhythmias

- Define bradyarrhythmias and assess heart rate in clinical context.
- Differentiate sinus bradycardia, junctional escape rhythms, and ventricular escape rhythms.
- Recognize first-degree, second-degree, and third-degree AV block.
- Differentiate Mobitz type I from Mobitz type II second-degree AV block.
- Identify high-grade AV block and complete AV dissociation.
- Assess PR intervals, dropped QRS complexes, and P-wave-to-QRS relationships.
- Differentiate blocked premature atrial complexes from true AV block.
- Explain the differences between AV nodal and infranodal conduction disease.
- Identify reversible causes, including medications, ischemia, electrolyte disturbances, and increased vagal tone.
- Recognize symptomatic bradycardia and conduction abnormalities requiring urgent evaluation.
- Explain the principles of temporary pacing and permanent pacemaker assessment.

# 2.13 Sinus Node Dysfunction

- Define sinus node dysfunction (SND) and describe impaired impulse generation or propagation.
- Recognize sinus bradycardia, sinus pauses, sinus arrest, and sinoatrial exit block.
- Explain chronotropic incompetence and its clinical significance.
- Recognize tachycardia-bradycardia syndrome.
- Differentiate intrinsic sinus node disease from physiologic bradycardia and medication effects.
- Describe the effects of aging, fibrosis, ischemia, and structural heart disease.
- Correlate rhythm abnormalities with syncope, presyncope, fatigue, and exercise intolerance.
- Explain the role of ambulatory ECG monitoring and exercise testing.
- Recognize the importance of symptom-rhythm correlation.
- Describe the principles of pacemaker evaluation in appropriately selected symptomatic patients.

# 2.14 Integrated Arrhythmia Diagnosis

- Apply a systematic approach using heart rate, regularity, QRS width, atrial activity, and AV relationships.
- Differentiate regular from irregular tachycardias.
- Classify tachycardias as narrow-complex or wide-complex.
- Evaluate P-wave morphology and PR/RP relationships.
- Differentiate atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, focal atrial tachycardia, AVNRT, and AVRT.
- Distinguish SVT with aberrancy from VT using established criteria and clinical context.
- Recognize PACs, PVCs, ventricular couplets, and nonsustained ventricular arrhythmias.
- Identify sinus node dysfunction and clinically significant AV block.
- Integrate ECG findings with symptoms, medications, electrolytes, and structural heart disease.
- Document the rhythm, supporting evidence, differential diagnosis, and limitations.
- Escalate potentially life-threatening findings for urgent clinical assessment.

# 2.15 ECG Quality and Diagnostic Limitations

- Recognize baseline wander, muscle artifact, electrical interference, and lead-placement errors.
- Differentiate artifact from genuine atrial and ventricular arrhythmias.
- Understand how filtering and recording quality affect waveform interpretation.
- Recognize limitations of automated ECG interpretation.
- Verify clinically important interval measurements when necessary.
- Identify challenges in QT measurement during irregular rhythms and wide-QRS states.
- Avoid definitive conclusions when ECG evidence is insufficient.
- Clearly document uncertainty and recommend additional assessment when appropriate.

# 2.16 Competency and Knowledge Assessment

- Perform systematic 12-lead ECG interpretation.
- Measure PR, QRS, QT, and QTc intervals accurately.
- Recognize atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, AVNRT, AVRT, and focal atrial tachycardia.
- Differentiate SVT from VT using appropriate ECG and clinical evidence.
- Identify PACs, PVCs, VT, and VF.
- Recognize bradyarrhythmias, AV block, and sinus node dysfunction.
- Explain the electrophysiologic mechanisms underlying identified rhythms.
- Distinguish definitive findings from suggestive findings and uncertainty.
- Identify ECG findings requiring urgent clinical escalation.
- Avoid inventing ECG findings or making unsupported diagnoses.

## Expected Learning Outcome

Demonstrate the ability to interpret 12-lead ECGs systematically, identify common and life-threatening arrhythmias, differentiate supraventricular from ventricular rhythms, recognize bradyarrhythmias and sinus node dysfunction, and explain the underlying electrophysiologic mechanisms and clinical significance.
