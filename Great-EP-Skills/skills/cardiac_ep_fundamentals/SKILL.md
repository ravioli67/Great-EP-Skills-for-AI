# 1. Cardiac Electrophysiology Fundamentals

## 1.1 Cardiac Conduction System Anatomy
- Identify the SA node, AV node, bundle of His, bundle branches, and Purkinje fibers.
- Explain the normal sequence of cardiac electrical activation.
- Describe AV nodal delay and its physiologic purpose.
- Differentiate specialized conduction tissue from working myocardium.
- Explain accessory pathways, bundle branch block, and fascicular block.
- Describe autonomic regulation of cardiac conduction.

## 1.2 Normal Sinus Rhythm
- Define normal sinus rhythm and its ECG characteristics.
- Identify sinus P waves preceding QRS complexes.
- Assess heart rate, rhythm regularity, and AV relationships.
- Calculate heart rate using standard ECG methods.
- Differentiate sinus rhythm, sinus bradycardia, sinus tachycardia, and sinus arrhythmia.
- Recognize sinus pauses, sinus arrest, and sinoatrial exit block.

## 1.3 ECG Waveforms
- Explain atrial depolarization and P-wave formation.
- Explain ventricular depolarization and QRS morphology.
- Explain ventricular repolarization and T-wave formation.
- Identify PR segments, ST segments, J points, and U waves.
- Recognize abnormal waveforms associated with conduction disorders, ischemia, and electrolyte disturbances.
- Differentiate primary from secondary repolarization abnormalities.

## 1.4 ECG Intervals and Measurements
- Measure PR interval, QRS duration, QT interval, and QTc.
- Understand standard ECG calibration and grid measurements.
- Recognize the usual adult PR interval of 120–200 ms.
- Identify narrow and wide QRS complexes.
- Calculate QTc using Bazett and Fridericia formulas.
- Explain the limitations of QT correction at extreme heart rates.
- Recognize factors associated with QT prolongation and torsades de pointes.
- Assess ECG rate, rhythm, axis, and interval abnormalities systematically.

## 1.5 Cardiac Action Potentials
- Describe fast-response action potentials in atrial and ventricular myocytes.
- Explain slow-response action potentials in SA and AV nodal cells.
- Identify action-potential phases and their associated ionic currents.
- Explain the roles of sodium, calcium, and potassium channels.
- Describe spontaneous phase 4 depolarization and pacemaker activity.
- Explain the electrophysiologic effects of antiarrhythmic medications and electrolyte disturbances.

## 1.6 Depolarization and Repolarization
- Define depolarization, repolarization, and resting membrane potential.
- Correlate atrial depolarization with the P wave.
- Correlate ventricular depolarization with the QRS complex.
- Correlate ventricular repolarization with the T wave.
- Explain electrical vectors and ECG waveform polarity.
- Differentiate cellular electrical activity from mechanical contraction.

## 1.7 Refractory Periods
- Define absolute, effective, and relative refractory periods.
- Explain sodium-channel recovery and tissue excitability.
- Describe the relationship between action-potential duration and refractoriness.
- Explain how premature impulses interact with refractory tissue.
- Describe the role of refractoriness in conduction block and reentry.

## 1.8 Conduction Velocity
- Define conduction velocity and its determinants.
- Compare conduction through atrial tissue, AV nodal tissue, and the His-Purkinje system.
- Explain the roles of sodium-channel availability and gap junctions.
- Differentiate conduction delay from conduction block.
- Describe how ischemia, fibrosis, and scar tissue slow conduction.
- Explain the relationship between conduction velocity and reentrant arrhythmias.

## 1.9 Automaticity, Excitability, and Conductivity
- Define automaticity as spontaneous impulse generation.
- Define excitability as the ability to respond to a stimulus.
- Define conductivity as the ability to propagate an electrical impulse.
- Explain the role of subsidiary pacemakers and overdrive suppression.
- Describe how autonomic stimulation affects automaticity.
- Differentiate abnormalities of impulse formation from abnormalities of impulse conduction.

## 1.10 Reentrant Arrhythmia Mechanisms
- Define reentry and explain how circulating impulses sustain tachycardia.
- Explain unidirectional block, slow conduction, and tissue recovery.
- Describe the roles of conduction velocity, circuit length, and refractory period.
- Differentiate anatomic and functional reentry.
- Explain excitable gaps, entrainment, and pacing-induced termination.
- Describe AVNRT, AVRT, atrial flutter, and scar-related ventricular tachycardia.
- Explain how catheter ablation interrupts reentrant circuits.

## 1.11 Focal Arrhythmia Mechanisms
- Define focal arrhythmias and centrifugal activation.
- Differentiate enhanced automaticity, abnormal automaticity, and triggered activity.
- Explain early and delayed afterdepolarizations.
- Describe focal atrial tachycardia and focal ventricular arrhythmias.
- Recognize the limitations of surface ECG in determining the precise mechanism.
- Explain the role of activation mapping in identifying focal sources.

## 1.12 Focal Versus Reentrant Arrhythmias
- Compare initiation, maintenance, activation sequence, and termination.
- Explain how programmed stimulation helps differentiate mechanisms.
- Interpret warm-up, cool-down, resetting, and entrainment findings.
- Differentiate AVNRT, AVRT, focal atrial tachycardia, and macroreentrant rhythms.
- Integrate surface ECG, intracardiac electrograms, and pacing responses.
- Recognize that no single ECG feature independently establishes every arrhythmia mechanism.

## 1.13 Basic Intracardiac Electrophysiology
- Identify common intracardiac recording locations.
- Recognize atrial, His, and ventricular electrograms.
- Define AH and HV intervals.
- Explain programmed stimulation and refractory-period testing.
- Describe capture, fusion, concealed conduction, and entrainment.
- Explain how intracardiac recordings support arrhythmia diagnosis.

## 1.14 Autonomic and Pharmacologic Effects
- Explain sympathetic and parasympathetic regulation of cardiac electrophysiology.
- Describe the electrophysiologic effects of beta-blockers and calcium-channel blockers.
- Explain sodium-channel and potassium-channel blocker effects.
- Describe adenosine and digoxin effects on conduction.
- Recognize medication-related bradycardia, AV block, QRS widening, and QT prolongation.
- Explain how electrolyte disturbances and drug interactions modify arrhythmia risk.

## 1.15 ECG Interpretation and Clinical Safety
- Apply a systematic ECG interpretation workflow.
- Assess rate, rhythm, axis, intervals, morphology, and repolarization.
- Recognize artifact, lead-placement errors, and automated interpretation limitations.
- Correlate ECG findings with symptoms and clinical context.
- Recognize potentially life-threatening rhythms and conduction abnormalities.
- Communicate uncertainty and escalate urgent findings appropriately.

## 1.16 Competency and Knowledge Assessment
- Interpret fundamental ECG waveforms and intervals.
- Explain cardiac action potentials and refractory periods.
- Differentiate automaticity, triggered activity, and reentry.
- Explain the mechanisms of focal and reentrant arrhythmias.
- Apply electrophysiologic concepts to clinical cases.
- Demonstrate accurate measurements and evidence-based reasoning.
- Avoid inventing ECG findings or making unsupported diagnostic conclusions.

## Expected Learning Outcome
Demonstrate foundational knowledge of cardiac conduction, ECG interpretation, action-potential physiology, refractory behavior, conduction velocity, and arrhythmia mechanisms as preparation for advanced ECG interpretation and clinical cardiac electrophysiology.
