# 8. Clinical Arrhythmia Management

## 8.1 Assessment of Symptomatic Palpitations
- Define palpitations and identify common arrhythmic and nonarrhythmic causes.
- Characterize symptom onset, duration, frequency, triggers, and associated symptoms.
- Assess for chest pain, dyspnea, dizziness, presyncope, and syncope.
- Review cardiac history, medications, stimulant use, and relevant family history.
- Evaluate heart rate, blood pressure, oxygenation, and hemodynamic stability.
- Interpret available 12-lead ECGs and rhythm-monitoring recordings.
- Recognize atrial fibrillation, SVT, ventricular arrhythmias, and symptomatic ectopy.
- Differentiate arrhythmic palpitations from anxiety, anemia, thyroid disorders, and other potential causes.
- Explain the role of Holter monitoring, event monitors, wearable ECGs, and implantable loop recorders.
- Identify red flags requiring urgent clinical assessment.
- Correlate symptoms with documented rhythm abnormalities whenever possible.
- Document the differential diagnosis and appropriate follow-up plan.

## 8.2 Evaluation of Syncope Potentially Related to Arrhythmias
- Define syncope and differentiate it from presyncope and other causes of transient loss of consciousness.
- Obtain a focused history of the event, circumstances, prodrome, recovery, and witness observations.
- Identify warning features, including exertional syncope, palpitations before collapse, and syncope without warning.
- Review structural heart disease, previous arrhythmias, and family history of sudden cardiac death.
- Assess vital signs, cardiovascular examination, and 12-lead ECG findings.
- Recognize bradyarrhythmias, advanced AV block, VT, and other potential arrhythmic causes.
- Consider reflex syncope, orthostatic hypotension, and noncardiac causes.
- Explain the role of telemetry, ambulatory monitoring, and implantable loop recorders.
- Describe when echocardiography and additional cardiac investigations may be indicated.
- Recognize high-risk presentations requiring urgent assessment and monitoring.
- Avoid attributing syncope to an arrhythmia without adequate supporting evidence.

## 8.3 Sudden Cardiac Death Risk Assessment
- Explain the mechanisms and clinical significance of sudden cardiac death (SCD).
- Identify major risk factors, including structural heart disease, prior myocardial infarction, and inherited arrhythmia syndromes.
- Assess history of sustained VT, ventricular fibrillation, and unexplained syncope.
- Explain the role of left ventricular ejection fraction in selected risk assessments.
- Recognize the relevance of cardiac MRI, scar assessment, and family history where appropriate.
- Describe the significance of nonsustained VT and other ventricular arrhythmia markers in specific populations.
- Explain how genetic testing may contribute to selected inherited arrhythmia evaluations.
- Differentiate primary prevention from secondary prevention of SCD.
- Explain the role of ICDs in appropriately selected patients.
- Recognize that no single test reliably predicts every episode of sudden cardiac death.
- Apply current guidelines and individualized specialist assessment.

## 8.4 Antiarrhythmic Medication Mechanisms
- Explain how antiarrhythmic medications modify impulse formation, conduction, and refractoriness.
- Describe the Vaughan Williams classification and its limitations.
- Explain Class I sodium-channel blocker effects on cardiac conduction.
- Differentiate Class IA, IB, and IC drug effects.
- Describe Class II beta-blockers and their effects on sympathetic stimulation.
- Explain Class III potassium-channel blocker effects on repolarization.
- Describe Class IV non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker effects on AV nodal conduction.
- Recognize the roles of adenosine and other agents outside the traditional classification.
- Explain how drug selection depends on arrhythmia type, ventricular function, comorbidities, and contraindications.
- Recognize proarrhythmia and the potential for medications to worsen existing rhythm disorders.
- Assess relevant renal function, hepatic function, electrolytes, and drug interactions.
- Explain the role of ECG monitoring and specialist review during treatment.

## 8.5 Medication-Related QT Prolongation
- Define the QT interval and corrected QT interval (QTc).
- Explain why QT duration must be interpreted in relation to heart rate and QRS duration.
- Recognize medications associated with acquired QT prolongation.
- Identify risk factors, including hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, bradycardia, and interacting medications.
- Explain the relationship between delayed ventricular repolarization and torsades de pointes.
- Recognize that QTc values around or above 500 ms are associated with increased torsades risk, although individual risk varies.
- Explain the limitations of QT correction formulas at extreme heart rates.
- Recognize that bundle branch block and ventricular pacing complicate QT interpretation.
- Describe appropriate review of medications, electrolytes, and reversible contributors.
- Identify concerning QT prolongation or ventricular ectopy requiring prompt clinical assessment.
- Explain why medication changes must consider the indication, alternative therapies, and clinical risk.
- Document QT/QTc measurements, measurement method, and relevant clinical context.

## 8.6 Anticoagulation in Atrial Fibrillation
- Explain how atrial fibrillation promotes thrombus formation and increases embolic stroke risk.
- Describe the role of oral anticoagulation in reducing thromboembolic events.
- Differentiate direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) from vitamin K antagonists.
- Explain the role of warfarin monitoring when indicated.
- Assess renal function, hepatic function, bleeding history, and potential drug interactions.
- Recognize the importance of appropriate anticoagulant selection and dosing.
- Explain that antiplatelet therapy is not an equivalent substitute for anticoagulation when anticoagulation is indicated for AF-related stroke prevention.
- Describe how mechanical heart valves and moderate-to-severe rheumatic mitral stenosis affect anticoagulant selection.
- Explain periprocedural anticoagulation considerations for cardioversion and catheter ablation.
- Recognize that anticoagulation decisions after rhythm control depend on thromboembolic risk, not solely on apparent maintenance of sinus rhythm.
- Assess adherence, bleeding concerns, and patient preferences.
- Follow current guidelines and individualized prescribing decisions.

## 8.7 Stroke Risk Assessment in Atrial Fibrillation
- Explain the relationship between atrial fibrillation and ischemic stroke.
- Identify the components of the CHA2DS2-VASc score.
- Recognize congestive heart failure, hypertension, age, diabetes, prior stroke or thromboembolism, vascular disease, and sex category as score components.
- Apply validated stroke-risk assessment methods according to current guidelines.
- Recognize that guideline recommendations and preferred scoring approaches may vary by region.
- Differentiate stroke-risk assessment from bleeding-risk assessment.
- Explain the role of tools such as HAS-BLED in identifying modifiable bleeding risk factors.
- Avoid using bleeding scores alone to automatically withhold indicated anticoagulation.
- Reassess stroke risk as the patient's clinical circumstances change.
- Explain shared decision-making regarding anticoagulation benefits and risks.
- Document the risk assessment and rationale for the management plan.

## 8.8 Indications for Electrical Cardioversion
- Differentiate synchronized electrical cardioversion from unsynchronized defibrillation.
- Identify tachyarrhythmias for which electrical cardioversion may be appropriate.
- Recognize hemodynamic instability attributable to a tachyarrhythmia as an indication for urgent synchronized cardioversion when appropriate.
- Explain the role of cardioversion in selected cases of atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, and regular tachycardias.
- Assess rhythm type, symptom duration, anticoagulation status, and thromboembolic risk before elective cardioversion.
- Explain the role of transesophageal echocardiography or an established anticoagulation strategy in selected AF cases.
- Describe the importance of sedation, monitoring, and appropriate airway support for elective procedures.
- Explain why synchronization to the QRS complex is important for organized tachyarrhythmias.
- Recognize circumstances requiring immediate treatment without delay for elective investigations.
- Explain post-cardioversion rhythm monitoring and recurrence assessment.
- Follow current resuscitation protocols and institutional procedures.

## 8.9 Hemodynamically Unstable Arrhythmias
- Recognize hypotension, altered mental status, shock, ischemic chest discomfort, and acute heart failure as signs of instability.
- Assess whether the arrhythmia is responsible for the patient's hemodynamic compromise.
- Activate emergency response systems and obtain immediate clinical assistance.
- Initiate appropriate monitoring and assess airway, breathing, circulation, and pulse.
- Recognize pulseless VT and VF as cardiac arrest rhythms requiring CPR and prompt defibrillation.
- Differentiate pulseless electrical activity and asystole from shockable rhythms.
- Explain the role of synchronized cardioversion for unstable tachyarrhythmias with a pulse when indicated.
- Recognize when severe symptomatic bradycardia requires urgent treatment and pacing assessment.
- Follow current advanced life support algorithms and local protocols.
- Identify and address reversible causes, including ischemia, electrolyte abnormalities, hypoxia, and medication toxicity.
- Reassess rhythm and perfusion following each intervention.
- Document interventions, response, and escalation of care.

## 8.10 Arrhythmia Management Planning
- Develop management plans collaboratively with the electrophysiology and wider clinical team.
- Define the rhythm diagnosis, supporting evidence, and relevant differential diagnoses.
- Incorporate symptom burden, arrhythmia frequency, structural heart disease, and patient preferences.
- Consider observation, medication, catheter ablation, device therapy, or other interventions when appropriate.
- Review potential treatment benefits, risks, contraindications, and alternatives.
- Incorporate stroke prevention and anticoagulation decisions for patients with atrial fibrillation.
- Establish a monitoring plan for treatment efficacy and medication toxicity.
- Define escalation criteria for recurrent symptoms or clinical deterioration.
- Coordinate follow-up, specialist referrals, and patient education.
- Document the agreed management strategy and unresolved clinical questions.

## 8.11 Clinical Documentation and Safety
- Record symptoms, vital signs, ECG findings, investigations, and relevant medical history.
- Distinguish confirmed arrhythmias from suspected mechanisms.
- Document medication indications, contraindications, and monitoring requirements.
- Recognize uncertainty in rhythm interpretation and seek expert review when needed.
- Prioritize emergency assessment for unstable arrhythmias and high-risk syncope.
- Use current clinical guidelines and institution-approved protocols.
- Avoid delaying emergency treatment while pursuing nonessential diagnostic information.
- Protect patient confidentiality and communicate critical findings promptly.

## 8.12 Competency and Knowledge Assessment
- Assess symptomatic palpitations and possible arrhythmic syncope.
- Explain sudden cardiac death risk factors and prevention strategies.
- Describe antiarrhythmic medication mechanisms and proarrhythmic risks.
- Recognize QT prolongation and associated torsades risk.
- Assess stroke risk and anticoagulation considerations in atrial fibrillation.
- Explain indications and safety principles for electrical cardioversion.
- Recognize and respond to hemodynamically unstable arrhythmias.
- Develop evidence-based arrhythmia management plans collaboratively.
- Communicate clinical findings, uncertainty, and escalation needs accurately.
- Work within professional scope and current clinical guidelines.

## Expected Learning Outcome
Demonstrate foundational competence in clinical arrhythmia assessment, sudden cardiac death risk evaluation, antiarrhythmic pharmacology, QT-related safety, AF stroke prevention, cardioversion, emergency arrhythmia recognition, and collaborative management planning.
